---
title: Pyrite
formula: FeS2
found: >-
  The most common sulfide mineral: in hydrothermal veins, in coal seams and black shales, in massive
  sulfide deposits like Rio Tinto in Spain, and as a trace in most rocks. The perfect brassy cubes in
  museums come from Navajún in La Rioja, where they grew in a marl bed. Its color and shine earned it
  the name fool's gold.
mined: >-
  For centuries pyrite was mined for its sulfur, roasted to sulfur dioxide and made into sulfuric acid,
  above all at Rio Tinto and Tharsis in Spain. Today most sulfur is recovered from oil and gas, so
  pyrite is mined for the gold and copper it carries, and otherwise is waste rock that must be kept
  from the air and rain, because it weathers to sulfuric acid and stains streams below old mines
  orange.
---
The name is from the Greek for fire: struck against steel it throws sparks, and it fired the wheellock
guns of the sixteenth century. Gold is soft and streaks yellow; pyrite is brittle and streaks
greenish black. In the structure the sulfur atoms come in pairs, bonded to each other, and iron sits
between the pairs in the same arrangement as the sodium and chlorine in halite.
