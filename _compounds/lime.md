---
title: Lime
formula: CaO
found: >-
  Almost never as a mineral, because it takes up water and carbon dioxide from the air within days and
  turns back into calcium hydroxide and calcite. It has been found in limestone baked by lava at
  Vesuvius and in the ash of burning coal seams. Nearly all the lime in the world is made, not found.
mined: >-
  Not mined but burned: limestone (calcite) is quarried, crushed to lumps and heated to 900 to 1,100 °C
  in vertical shaft kilns or long rotary kilns, which drives off the carbon dioxide and leaves
  quicklime. Steelmaking takes the largest share, as a flux that pulls silica, phosphorus and sulfur
  out of the molten iron. The rest goes to water treatment, flue-gas scrubbing, sugar refining, soil
  and mortar.
---
A lump of lime heated in a flame of hydrogen and oxygen glows a brilliant white, and before electric
light this was the limelight of the Victorian stage. Lime mortar is the oldest cement: slaked with
water to calcium hydroxide and mixed with sand, it sets by taking carbon dioxide back from the air and
turning into calcite again, closing the circle it started from. The crystal has the same chessboard
structure as halite.
