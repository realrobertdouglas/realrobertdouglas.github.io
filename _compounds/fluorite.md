---
title: Fluorite
formula: CaF2
found: >-
  In hydrothermal veins and in limestone beside galena, sphalerite and barite, as cubes and octahedra
  in purple, green, blue and yellow. The classic localities are the North Pennines and Derbyshire in
  England, where the banded purple Blue John is cut into vases, and Cave-in-Rock in southern Illinois.
  Most of the world's supply now comes from China, Mexico and Mongolia.
mined: >-
  Open pit and underground. The ore, called fluorspar, is crushed and sorted by density into
  metallurgical grade, which goes into steel furnaces as a flux that makes the slag flow (the name is
  from the Latin for flowing), and by flotation into acid grade of over 97% fluorite, which is turned
  into hydrofluoric acid for refrigerants, non-stick coatings and the cryolite baths that aluminium is
  smelted in.
---
The element fluorine and the word fluorescence are both named after it; George Stokes coined the
second in 1852 after watching a fluorite crystal glow blue under ultraviolet light. The calcium atoms
sit at the corners and face centers of the cube, and fluorine fills every one of the eight tetrahedral
holes between them, an arrangement so common that it is called the fluorite structure wherever it
turns up, in uranium dioxide fuel pellets and zirconia among others.
