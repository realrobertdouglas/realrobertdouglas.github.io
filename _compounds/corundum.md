---
title: Corundum
formula: Al2O3
found: >-
  In metamorphic rocks rich in aluminium and poor in silica, such as marble and gneiss, and in the
  river gravels that weathered out of them. Red corundum is ruby, from Mogok in Myanmar and Montepuez
  in Mozambique; every other color is sapphire, from Sri Lanka, Kashmir, Madagascar and Montana.
  A trace of chromium makes the red, iron and titanium the blue.
mined: >-
  Gem corundum mostly comes from gravels, dug by hand or with small excavators and washed in sluices
  so the heavy crystals stay behind. Emery, an impure corundum, is quarried on Naxos and in Turkey
  for sandpaper and grinding wheels. Almost all other corundum is made: aluminium oxide from bauxite
  is fused in electric arc furnaces for abrasives, or melted drop by drop in a flame and grown into
  boules by the Verneuil process, invented in 1902, for watch jewels, lasers and sapphire windows.
---
Corundum is 9 on the Mohs scale, harder than anything natural except diamond. The first laser, built
by Theodore Maiman in 1960, was a rod of synthetic ruby with silvered ends. The structure is the same
as hematite's: oxygens in close-packed layers with aluminium in two-thirds of the octahedral holes between them.
