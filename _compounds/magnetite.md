---
title: Magnetite
formula: Fe3O4
found: >-
  With hematite in banded iron formations, and on its own in a few huge igneous ore bodies, above all
  Kiruna in northern Sweden. The taconite of Minnesota's Mesabi Range is magnetite scattered through
  hard chert. It is also the black sand on many beaches and a trace in nearly every igneous rock.
  Lodestone is magnetite that has been magnetized, probably by lightning.
mined: >-
  Kiruna is mined underground by sublevel caving, the ore drawn off from a level 1,365 m down; the
  taconite pits of the Mesabi are open pit. Because magnetite is strongly magnetic the ore, often only
  25 to 30% iron, is crushed, ground fine and run past magnetic drums that pull the magnetite out of
  the waste. The concentrate is rolled into pellets and fired for blast furnaces and direct reduction.
---
Lodestone was the first compass needle, and the word magnet comes from Magnesia in Greece, where it
was found. Each formula unit has one iron(II) and two iron(III). One iron(III) sits in a small
tetrahedral hole in the oxygen packing; the other and the iron(II) share the larger octahedral holes,
so electrons hop between them and the mineral conducts, which is why it is black and metallic rather
than red like hematite.
