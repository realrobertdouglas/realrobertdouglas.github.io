---
title: Rutile
formula: TiO2
found: >-
  Scattered through granite, gneiss and schist as small dark-red crystals, and concentrated by the
  sea into heavy mineral sands: Richards Bay in South Africa, the east coast of Australia, Sierra Leone
  and the beaches of Kerala. Golden needles of rutile inside quartz are sold as rutilated quartz, and
  fine needles of it inside corundum make the six-rayed star in a star sapphire.
mined: >-
  Dredged from beach and dune sands by floating dredges or dug with excavators. The sand is run down
  spirals that separate the heavy grains from quartz, then magnets take out the ilmenite and
  electrostatic separators pick the rutile from the zircon. Most of it is chlorinated and burned back
  to titanium dioxide pigment, the white in paint, paper and toothpaste; some goes to titanium metal
  and to the coating on welding rods.
---
Rutile bends light more strongly than almost any other mineral, which is why fine particles of
titanium dioxide scatter light so completely and make the whitest pigment there is. The same compound
also crystallizes as anatase and brookite; rutile is the stable form.
