Fear and reverence get mixed up. They are not the same thing.

Reverence means you know you are not the center of everything. You can be quiet. You can admit you are wrong. You can treat people better because of what you believe.

Fear is different. Fear asks, "What will get me in trouble?" It watches the room. It copies the right words. It stays quiet so nobody will think it is weak or disloyal.

A lot of what gets called reverence is just fear in church clothes.

## Where it shows

You can see it in the rules. Rules are not the problem. Every group has lines. The problem is when the lines become the whole point. People learn which questions are safe. They learn which doubts make them look bad. They learn to sound certain even when they are not. After a while, the best performers rise, and the group starts treating the performance as faith.

You can see it in the leaders, too. If asking a hard question feels rude, bad teaching survives. If loyalty matters more than honesty, cruel people get cover. The sermon turns into a warning: stay afraid of hell, stay afraid of outsiders, stay afraid of being the one who does not fit.

## What the tradition says

That is not what the tradition actually says at its best. Jesus tells anxious people to stop being afraid. He gets angry at religious people who load heavy rules on others and skip justice and mercy. One New Testament letter says real love pushes fear out. If love and fear are fighting, fear is not the holy one.

This does not mean every serious Christian is fake. Discipline is not abuse. Awe is not panic. Some people pray, give, forgive, and tell the truth when it costs them. They do not need an audience.

## The test

The test is simple. Does the faith make someone harder to scare into cruelty, or easier? Does it make the circle of neighbors bigger, or smaller? Does it lead to repair, or mostly to blame?

Fear can copy the look of faith. It cannot copy the direction.
