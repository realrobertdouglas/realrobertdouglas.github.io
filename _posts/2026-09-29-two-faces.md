Two customers, same morning, same complaint. One leaves with the owner's cell number and an apology. The other leaves with a copy of the policy.

Nothing about the complaint was different. Nothing about the manners. The first one could cost the business something. The second one couldn't.

## Holding a name

A business that has built a name has something to lose, and from then on most of what it does is arranged around not losing it. That's what status is once you have it. Not a thing you enjoy. A thing you hold. And holding is fear.

A frightened business doesn't look at you. It looks past you, at whoever you can reach. The friend of the owner. The account that pays for the quarter. The person with a following. The inspector's brother-in-law. It's adding up what you could do to the name if it sent you away unhappy, and it's doing that before you've finished your first sentence.

## Two faces

Fear makes it two-faced.

Upward, it's gracious. The manager appears. The exception is found. The policy turns out to have always had room in it. Someone says "of course" and means it, because the alternative is a phone call from somebody they'd rather not hear from.

Downward, it's cold. The policy is read out. Nobody is available. The tone is polite and the door is closed. The policy is what a business says to people who can't hurt it.

The difficult customer with reach gets a call from the owner. The difficult customer without it gets the door. Same difficulty. Different face.

## It's the same people

The part that stays with me is that it isn't two staffs. It's one staff, and they switch. The person who read you the policy at ten is finding an exception for someone else at eleven. They didn't change. The calculation did. And they learned the calculation by watching the owner make it.

That's the cost. Every time a business shows its cold face to someone who can't hurt it, someone is watching who can. The staff see both faces and learn which one is real. The customer with reach sees the door shut on someone else and understands, correctly, that it's the door they'd get the day their reach ran out.

## The other kind

There are businesses with a name that aren't afraid of losing it. They treat the two customers the same, and the same is well. Not because it pays, though it does. Because the name isn't something they hold. It's something they are.

You can tell the two apart in one look. Watch how they treat the person who can't hurt them. That's the face they'll show you, the day you can't either.
